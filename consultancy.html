How an engagement runs

Four steps. You can stop after any of them.

  1. A twenty-minute call

    Free. You describe what is eating the time; I tell you whether software fixes it. Sometimes the answer is that it doesn't, and I'd rather say so than sell you a project.

  2. A written scope with a fixed price

    What gets built, what it will and won't do, what I need from you, and what it costs. One page, no retainer to read it. If the scope is wrong you say so before anyone spends money.

  3. Build, with something visible each week

    Half up front, half on delivery. You get a working thing you can click every week rather than a status report. Changes inside the scope are included; changes outside it get priced before I start them.

  4. Handover, then care if you want it

    Source code, credentials and documentation are yours. I'll train whoever runs it. Monthly care covers hosting, backups, monitoring and updates, and you can cancel it any time without losing access to your own system.

Straight answers

What if the automation doesn't work?

Scope is fixed in writing before I start, including what “working” means. If I deliver something that doesn't meet it, I fix it at my cost. What I won't do is promise a business outcome I don't control — I can guarantee the software answers correctly, not that your market buys more.

Do I own what you build?

Yes. Code, data and accounts are yours on final payment. I don't hold your hosting hostage, and I'll hand over to another developer without friction if you move on.

Will you use my data to train something?

No. Your content is used to configure your system and nothing else. If your project involves customer data, I'll tell you exactly which third-party services touch it before we start, so you can decide.

How do you invoice, and in what currency?

Fixed price in USD or EUR, invoiced in two parts. Turkish clients can be invoiced in lira. Care plans are billed monthly.

What don't you take on?

Anything I'd be learning on your budget, native mobile apps, and projects where nobody on your side can make a decision. I'd rather refer you to someone better placed than deliver something mediocre slowly.
